ALGORITHMS / Cognitive Science & Neuroscience


M Class:           Programming Paradigms, Algorithms and Complexity
W Lab:             Multi-Paradigm Programming
F Discussion:    Intelligence, Computational Matter and Data Determinism

This week we study how Computer Science represents and implements thought in code.  We'll study this with examples of specific algorithms (e.g. search, sort) and general programming patterns (e.g. recursion).  More generally, we'll explore organizing computational thought through the lens of four different programming paradigms. 

We close the week with a review of the intellectual history of formalized systems of thought from Philosophy to Logic to Maths to Computer Science. Finally, we examine the phenomena of Consciousness and how it relates to both human and artificial intelligence.

NOTE: Although this list is long it only totals about an hour of viewing/reading time per class meeting.  You will be introduced to fundamental concepts of algorithms, programming paradigms and associated key theoretical concepts. Do not be intimidated - you are not expected to absorb all of this and there will be no coding expected.  Simply work through the videos one at a time while summarizing the high-level concepts.  Come to class on Monday with this foundation when we'll clarify and connect these diverse approaches to and aspects of computational thinking into a coherent whole. 

Monday Introduction:

These readings/videos introduce you to the concepts of Algorithms and Programming Paradigms as well as core theories and hardware architecture that underlie all modern digital computers. The main goal is to understand the many ways algorithms in particular (and intelligence in general) can be represented and implemented in software, hardware and more generally other mediums (e.g. from gears and organelles).

We cannot possibly cover every topic so we introduce on some of the more illustrative and fundamental concepts of algorithms and programming paradigms here. Although Python is a multi-paradigm language and can be programmed in all four models, in the following weeks we'll largely stick to a procedural style with object-oriented idioms where convenient.

Wednesday Lab:

The main video homework is a presenter summarizing her favorite class at Harvey Mudd College that studied various programming paradigms. Although most people routinely only program in 1-2 languages, exposure to concepts from other languages is generally acknowledged to make for better thinkers with a more expressive palette of cognitive tools. In addition, useful concepts from functional programming (e.g. lambda functions and mappings) have spread into traditionally non-functional languages like Python and Java.

Examining algorithmic thinking from biology rather than silicon, the second video shows how we are beginning to mathematically model algorithmic thinking in humans at high-level abstractions.

Friday Discussion:

Now that we understand a bit of how both artificial and human intelligence is implemented and functions, we have a base to question its relation with Consciousness.  We do so from the perspective of several different thinkers including physicist Max Tegmark, philosopher Daniel Dennett and neuroscientist Giulio Tononi.
