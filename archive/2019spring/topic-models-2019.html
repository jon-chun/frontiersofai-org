Modeling - Economics, Environmental Studies and Cognitive Science M Class:            Models and Game Theory W Lab:            Probabilistic Models, Economic and Climate Models F Discussion:    Global Warming, Public Policy and Economic Inequality

We've crammed a lot of relatively dense technical material into the last two weeks (Statistics, Data Science and Machine Learning), so this week we'll take a pause to reinforce and build upon some of these concepts.  Abstracting out some of the statistical/programming details allows us to sample from a wide diversity of more complex model types in various interesting domains and applications.

One theme repeated throughout our course is the idea that reality (as such) is extremely information dense, complex and stochastic in nature.  Although AI systems constantly improve with more data, processing power and better algorithms they (like their human counterparts) struggle to understand the world and everything in it with accuracy, reliability and generalizability.

This week, we focus on the diversity of models and the process of utilizing model across a variety of domains and at various levels of abstractions.  Many of these models come from the toolboxes of Computer Science, Physics and Engineering and Statistics/Math. This will hopefully improve our wisdom in optimizing model choice, customization, implementation, evaluation and constant refinement. The study of models this weeks unifies many topics we studied earlier from data to machine learning. The ability to think critically and intelligently about models and all they entail (data, algorithms, training, scoring, visualizations, etc) improves your ability to observe, understand, predict and control the world at large as well as the world within. 

If you have any questions about material from the last two topics please use this week of relative respite to meet in office hours. We can clarify any points of confusion together and personalize explanations that we don't have time in regular class time to explore.
