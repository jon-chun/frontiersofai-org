Week Fourteen: Artificial Intelligence

Before our final project, we'll look at what some speculate may be humankind's 'last great invention': Artificial Intelligence (AI). Neuroscience provides the only model of advanced intelligence in our universe, and recent advances in AI have developed neural nets modeled on the neural pathways of the brain. We'll explore original Artificial Neuron (AN) inspirations from neuroscience as well as the growing cross-pollination between AI and Neuroscience. Then we'll discuss what it means as our computers increasingly become 'smarter' than us by more metrics in a growing number of diverse fields (including playing Go, protein folding, poker, language translation, programming, medical diagnosis, writing, and art).

Next we'll reflect again on the nature of intelligence and consciousness. Can machines "think" and will we be able to model human consciousness in silica? Finally, we'll conclude with the international AI arms race, as well as a discussion of a possible existential threat. What solutions can we offer from the perspective of the liberal arts and our humanistic tradition? Should we have a greater say, earlier, in the development and implementation of AI?
