Topic: Evolving Life

Home » 2021 Archive » Topic: Evolving Life
Created: 2021-11-10 • Modified: 2025-11-18 • Archived from programminghumanity.wordpress.com

If we view humans from a computational perspective, we can see living cells as information processing systems that execute DNA code to create proteins using a complex biochemical operating system. We are built from a massively parallel computational system based on carbon and chemistry rather than silicon and electromagnetism.

This week, we explore biology as an information processing system. We'll understand why a computational model of cellular life is orders of magnitude more complex than any model we've seen so far. The information, behaviors, processing pathways and feedbacks in overlapping and interconnected systems create constantly shifting and dynamically self-modifying networks. These networks are embedded within each other, inter-operate alongside and between each other and run simultaneously with distinct signaling at different levels of resolution.

We also see how the unique challenges of bioinformatics require high-performance computational and large-scale statistical methods to study genomics--one of the original big data applications (which also includes astrophysics and experimental subatomic physics). Bioinformatics and computational biology have revolutionized our understanding of genetics, medicine and life thanks in large part to rapid advances in computer science: big data, computational power and improved algorithms. 

As in other weeks, we see how rapid progress in technology is poised to challenge the very nature of humanity.  Forty years ago, test tube baby technologies like IVF were new, controversial, and pushed the limits of medical ethics. Despite initial attempts to limit access to the procedure, today approximately 10% of all births in Denmark are born with assisted reproductive technology like IVF.

The fictional world depicted in the 1997 film Gattaca is fast becoming a reality.  For the cost of a few thousand dollars, parents can practice statistical 'soft' eugenics by fertilizing multiple eggs, testing each for desirable traits across a million SNPs, and selecting the 'best' one for implantation. How can society prevent a massive, permanent, and accelerating biological inequality from a man-made speciation event that could fundamentally separate the 'haves' from the 'have nots'?

This week also raises a number of questions about genetic engineering, precision medicine, creating deadly new bioagents (for academic, commercial and military ends), and the quest to "cure" aging. Once again, computation applied to a domain (that of life itself) results in possibilities that are both incredibly promising and terrifying.

Unfortunately, Datacamp does not offer concise exercises in bioinformatics, computational biology,  or genomics, and these approaches would also require a fair amount of domain knowledge. Instead, we'll continue our Datacamp exercises building a foundation in statistical thinking. We'll also begin some real-world applications of the methods you've learned, for example analyzing a TV dataset. Sentiment Analysis Miniproject

Rubric: An "A" project: 

  • Evaluates the different arcs for coherence/similarity. How similar are they? Where are the greatest points of discrepancy?
  • Compares the arcs with your preregistered version. Which model comes closest? What similarities are there? What are the largest differences?
  • Evaluates at least 4 peaks and valleys of the averaged arc. Compare at least two smoothing methods.
  • Evaluates the language around the approximate location of the crux (usually closer to the text in caps) to estimate whether it marks an emotional inflection point. Is it accurate in terms of intensity (how high or low it registers) esp. in comparison to other cruxes? Is it a "false crux" that fails to capture emotion due to some other element (i.e. irony, humor, etc). 
  • Compares the cruxes with those you chose. Did it register the ones you identified?
  • Demonstrates how the approach deepens your understanding of the text. What did it reveal that you missed? What did it miss that you feel it should have modeled?
  • Includes graphs/data/text to support your argument. Please include graphs/data pasted into the doc (or into google slides). These should include quotes from the text when you analyze each crux point.
