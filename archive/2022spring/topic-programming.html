Topic: Languages (Artificial and Natural)

Home » Spring 2022 Archive » Topic: Languages (Artificial and Natural)
Created: 2019-01-09 • Modified: 2025-10-02 • Archived from programminghumanity.wordpress.com

This week we study artificial (programming) languages and compare them to natural languages (English, Spanish, etc.). Borrowing from cognitive science and the philosophy of language, we'll see important ways language and thought influence and shape each other. In earlier weeks we've considered the ways our access to and interpretation of information/data can affect how we describe a problem and how we solve it. This week we'll see how artificial and natural languages can constrain how we describe and even think about reality.

We'll also compare different theories of how natural languages work. Theories of language, like theories of consciousness and  information processing, have implications for whether AI can fully understand and employ human language. First we'll explore the parallels between natural language and artificial languages (like Python) in terms of grammar rules, parsing and interpretation (e.g. how natural languages are 'fuzzy'). This also helps guide researchers along the most promising paths. You'll also learn how four major programming paradigms "think" and "describe" the same problem differently. Then we'll explore object-oriented programming (OOP) and its philosophical counterpart, object-oriented ontology (OOO).

Then, we'll focus on the big questions: How has Computation/AI changed the way we understand language, how we philosophically conceptualize our world, and how we theorize our cultural productions?

In Datacamp, you'll continue learning Python both this week and next. Many of the units spiral back to concepts already introduced, from Matplotlib to slicing. You will also employ the various kinds of logic and loops we first encountered in runestone.
