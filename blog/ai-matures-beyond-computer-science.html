AI Matures beyond Computer Science

Home » Blog » AI Matures beyond Computer Science
Posted by jonchunJuly 27, 2019

The University of Oxford is the latest world-class university to launch a major initiative to synthesize the study of AI with the Liberal Arts and broader scope of humanity’s concerns.

This announcement was preceded by Stanford’s launch this spring 2019 of their Institute for Human-Centered AI. Stanford’s HCA mission is ‘to advance AI research, education, policy, and practice to improve the human condition.” HCA is designed to creates cross-disciplinary links between experts in AI and all disciplines across the university from Medicine, Law, Social Sciences and the Humanities.

“Our goal is for Stanford HAI to become an interdisciplinary, global hub for AI thinkers, learners, researchers, developers, builders and users from academia, government and industry, as well as leaders and policymakers who want to understand and leverage AI’s impact and potential.”

MIT deserves first-(big)mover credit for bringing focus on the urgency, challenges and potential to synthesize AI with the Liberal Arts and various professions like medicine, law and public policy. In October 2018 they announced an initiative to build from the ground up a new $1 billion Schwarzman School of AI staffed with a balance between AI experts and those from a wide range of disciplines in the Liberal Arts and professional schools.

Princeton became the latest top US University to launch a major initiative to address the growing opportunity and challenges of a world increasingly driven by AI (Data Science is a subset of Machine Learning which is a subset of AI). This May 2019 the Schmidt DataX Fund was announced to cultivate a multidisciplinary approach between data-driven machine learning and virtually every discipline across campus. To quote from the Princeton announcement:

“Eric Schmidt noted that when he earned his undergraduate degree from Princeton in 1976, ‘I majored in electrical engineering, because computer science was barely an option. Now it’s the largest department at Princeton and data science has the potential to transform every discipline, and find solutions to profound societal problems. Wendy and I are excited to think about what will be possible when Princeton is able to gather students and faculty in one place, right at the center of campus, to discover now-unimaginable solutions for the future century.'”

Harvard has long recognized the growing role of technology in society has worked to cultivate both individual and coherent cross-disciplinary centers of excellence. Traditionally famous for disproportionately producing leaders in business, law and politics undergrads, Harvard has long had excellent math/physics departments. In more recent decades it has cultivated world-class departments in Engineering, CS and Health Sciences. Harvard’s innovative CS50 “Introduction to the Intellectual Enterprise of Computer Science and the Art of Programming” now vies with economics as the most popular undergrad course, has expanded to Yale and is Coursera’s most popular on-line course (>1million students).

The Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society was established in 1996 at Harvard Law School. Prof. Lawrence Lessig (a board member on my last startup) was the first faculty member to join and the Berkman Klein Center which has become a major interdisciplinary center to study the mutual effects between technology and society. To quote the centers web page:

We  bring together the sharpest, most thoughtful people from around the globe to tackle the biggest challenges presented by the Internet.

As an interdisciplinary, University-wide center with a global scope, we have an unparalleled track record of leveraging exceptional academic rigor to produce real- world impact. We pride ourselves on pushing the edges of scholarly research, building tools and platforms that break new ground, and fostering active networks across diverse communities. United by our commitment to the public interest, our vibrant, collaborative community of independent thinkers represents a wide range of philosophies and disciplines, making us a unique home for open-minded inquiry, debate, and experimentation.

In 2015 the Harvard Kennedy School and The Future Society together established The AI Initiative which “gathers students, researchers, alumni, faculty and experts from Harvard and beyond, interested in understanding the rise of Artificial Intelligence. It’s mission is to help shape the global AI policy framework.”

Yale is the sole exception among this slew of high-profile, big-dollar commitments at elite universities. This April 2019 Yale announced a modest interdisciplinary AI and Ethics workshop. This is unfortunate given family connections to the institution and the talent pool there (we had a solid CS intern from Yale in our last startup). Perhaps this is symptomatic of the larger questions around the future direction of the university, faltering alumni support and even dissent. Hopefully, the future will build upon the achievements of previous President Levin, many of which focused on promoting interdisciplinary and STEM education that is socially relevant, intellectually engaging and key to human progress. These initiatives included a $500m investment in five new science and engineering buildings, promotion of cross-disciplinary studies, reviving the Dean of Engineering post in 1994 and forming the School of Engineering and Applied Science (SEAS) in 2008.

Outside these elite institutions there are many more recent initiatives to integrate technology/AI with larger academic/social concerns among other top universities. Here is a sample of major academic initiatives focused all or in part on the wide-ranging effects of AI on society (apologies for any oversights):

  • Oxford Future of Humanity Institute (2006, recently more focused on AI since 2014)
  • UChicago Data Science for Social Good (2013) and Center for Data Science and Public Policy (2017)
  • Northwestern Data Science Initiative (2015)
  • Cambridge Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence (2015)
  • UC Berkeley Center for Human Compatible AI (2016)
  • Brown The Humanity Centered Robotics Initiative (2016)
  • USC Center for AI and Society (2016)
  • NYU AI Now Institute (2017)
  • CIFAR (Canadian Universities) AI & Society (2017)
  • CMU Block Center for Technology and Society (2018)
  • Cornell-r4 Applied AI Initiative (2018)
  • Georgetown Center for Security and Emerging Technology (2019)
  • Duke Center on Risk in Science and Society (201?)

These are only some most recent and highest profile examples of the growing awareness that technology, particularly AI, has a tremendous and growing role to play in virtually every aspect of our personal and social lives. The changes Artificial Intelligence auger for humanity are so profound it is central to what is being called the 4th Industrial Revolution. In the design and description of all these programs we can see the unanimous opinion that any solutions we find must involve a multidisciplinary approach that is reflected in university training.

The century+ academic tradition of isolated islands of specialization and resistance to change may explain why new interdisciplinary approaches are being instituted from the top-down in the form of new schools, centers and programs. Research collaboration across departments, especially in the Humanities, can still be difficult by traditional metrics. However, the rising popularity of interdisciplinary research (Digital Humanities, Data Science, AI, etc), cross-disciplinary programs (Global Studies, Environmental Studies, Synthetic Biology, CS+X, etc) and double/triple undergrad majors/concentrations often with quantitative skills like Computer Science illustrate the grassroots bottom up demand for change. In all these trends we see a desire for an more integrated approach to understand our increasingly technological, complex and interconnected world.

Our unique interdisciplinary courses in Programming Humanity and AI for the Humanities align with most of the initiatives above. Launched in 2017 at Kenyon College, we emphasize a Liberal Arts approach to Computational Studies and AI in particular via the Digital Humanities. Over the past several years we have witnessed an explosion in interest both within academia and beyond to encompass a more interdisciplinary view of AI to safeguard humanity. Our courses are designed to also help our students adapt, anticipate and thrive in a rapidly changing and unpredictable future. It has been exciting to be a part of this revolution.
