AIs without a Face

Home » Blog » AIs without a Face
Posted by jonchunAugust 13, 2019

Poker AI systems provide an interesting contrast between how humans traditionally deal with imperfect information game scenarios and how AI does. Over the past two years it’s been impressive watching card-playing AIs (CMU/Libratus, DeepMind/Hanabi, and now Facebook/Pluribus) evolve to learn better, quicker, and independently from humans. There are many opportunities to apply this AI to optimize negotiations of transactions, contracts and other deals that resemble imperfect information games (at least in part). Prof. Toumas Sandholm at CMU is looking into applying this technology to situations like cybersecurity, business negotiations, or medical planning

Imperfect information games are interesting due to inherit complexity as well as for involving and exposing a lot of human psychological traits. Many of these can be used by better players against weaker ones to gain an information and psychological advantage against humans. The New Yorker video below illustrates how poker play among sophisticated players operates at a mental level.

I find a stark contrast between the 2nd New Yorker video and the 1st Two Minute Paper video atop this post. In the New Yorker video the players become animated recalling the psychological narrative they experienced playing other humans. In contrast, the first video (starting at 3:58) shows quotes reflecting feelings of fatigue, awe and resignation human players felt playing the seemingly indomitable poker bot.
