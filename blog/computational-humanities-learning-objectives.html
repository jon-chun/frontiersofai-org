Computational Humanities Learning Objectives Draft

Home » Blog » Computational Humanities Learning Objectives
Created: 2019-05-26 • Status: draft

Train the Mind to think rigorously. Systematically breakdown a problem and logically reason through.

Better understanding and prediction of situations. Programmic models and mathematical multimodel thinking

More effective and persuasive communication via data, visualizations and narrative storytelling

Greatly expanded intellectual framework and conceptual vocabulary of universal truths and the practiced mental agility to fluidly generalize to many appropriate situations

Optimal decisions in the face of uncertainty. Perceive, quantify and control uncertainty

Identify, understand and correct for bias, cognitive errors and evolutionary heuristic errors in oneself and others from perception to cognition to statistical

Learn practical but undertaught key techniques to sophisticated your practical/applied quantitative thinking: when correlation implies causation, thinking probabilistically, factors replication crisis, statistical rhetoric

Expanded opportunities to affect change in the world

Understand, empathize, and connect with others

Learn grit by working on personally compelling but intentionally but realistically incomplete specified problems involving difficult technologies

Research, Discover and pursue a passion project by translating a social/intellectual passion into a class project

Prepare for future challenges a wide variety of technology presents society and work for the optimal outcome for humanity. This involves not only understanding the ethical issues but specific technical nature, challenges and interactions across a wide spectrum of technologies from AI to Synthetic Biology to Game Theory

Work towards developing a unified theory of everything (as best possible realizing edge cases and limitations) grounded in the science and technology across a wide spectrum of STEM, demonstrate the ability to think creatively, critically and with complex yet subtle integration both between the Liberal Arts and Technology as well as across multidisciplinary fields within each (no hand waving or homerism)

Personal and professional opportunities

Have a framework of specific tools that are the most important across key fields/functions in CS with a fundamental working understanding and clear guidance on how to pursue future learning/use

Become comfortable with the most valuable/common programming paradigms (proc, oop, fun, prob), programming (Python, syntax reading, structure, idioms), DSL (SQL, ReEx), systems (OS, DB, Web Servers), libraries (scikit-learn, XGBoost), frameworks (tf) and models (markov, bayesian probability graph models)
