Think Like a Computer Scientist

Home » Blog » Think Like a Computer Scientist
Posted by jonchunJanuary 24, 2019

Allen B. Downey is one of the most prolific, generous and multi-talented professors teaching undergraduate computer science today. He does a good job here as Defender of the Faith on behalf of Bayesian statistics. Today I got access to his newest book titled “Think Julia” co-authored with Ben Lauwens and published by O’Reilly Media.

Chapter 1 opens with an insight relevant to our week studying Algorithms and Programming. Downey and Lauwens echo was what we repeatedly emphasized in our class this week: we want to teach you how to think beyond the confines of any particular programming paradigm. In fact, we want to teach you mental models and computational methods you will find useful in generalizing beyond Computer Science. The authors elegantly express the multi-disciplinary and poly-cognitive nature of programming with this insight:

The goal of this book is to teach you to think like a computer scientist. This way of thinking combines some of the best features of mathematics, engineering, and natural science. Like mathematicians, computer scientists use formal languages to denote ideas (specifically computations). Like engineers, they design things, assembling components into systems and evaluating tradeoffs among alternatives. Like scientists, they observe the behavior of complex systems, form hypotheses, and test predictions.

I would add that like computer scientists, Humanity scholars and students learn how to intelligently apply all these practices to domains of expertise intimately related to the human experience.
