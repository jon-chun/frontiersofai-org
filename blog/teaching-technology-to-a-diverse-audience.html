Teaching Technology to a Diverse Audience Draft

Home » Blog » Teaching Technology to a Diverse Audience
Created: 2019-05-13 • Status: draft

One of the most challenging and enjoyable parts of teaching technologies like AI to a diverse class at a Small Liberal Arts College is constantly tuning the topics, content and abstraction levels to discover the most meaningful and interesting common ground.

Our Digital Humanities courses are explicitly designed to be inclusive in welcoming traditionally underrepresented groups as possible. Many of our students may be unfamiliar with or feel intimidated by the many STEMy topics we cover, so I default to the simpler worldviews and models when I cannot find a way to simultaneously speak at multiple levels of abstraction.

Our classes have always had a number of Math, Physics and other STEM students who are already familiar with both the practical and theoretical basis of our course. The practical include topics like linear algebra, statistics and basic programming. The theoretical include topics like Abstract Algebra and Group Theory.

To keep these students challenged I've usually suggested topics and resources that connect their personal passion. In particular, I try to identify areas that are intellectually interesting, have a high impact and that present opportunities of an evolving field. These include topics relevant to research and/or commercial focus like homomorphic encryption, network theory and synthetic immunology.

The STEM students in our class keep us grounded in the theoretical truths that underlie everything we discuss from Information Theory to Computational Complexity to Bayesian Networks. Even though we cannot cover such topics in depths, just their presence keeps an honesty and seriousness to our approach.

On the other hand, I feel a particular obligation to personally keep these students challenged in ways I cannot address in the mixed classroom of diverse students. Usually, these students need resources and guidance tailored to their unique backgrounds. In this post I'd like to address summer programming recommendations tailored to their needs.

One of the major challenges in teaching technology to diverse class is the wide range of backgrounds, interests and abilities. I am in constant problem solving mode trying to find innovative ways to match each particular student's unique situation to several options for finding interesting and high-impact intersection between technology and humanity.
