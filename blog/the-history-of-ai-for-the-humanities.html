The analyst industry produces commentary, forecasts, and conferences for interested parties like investment firms and large corporate customers. In the tech industry, they act as a quasi-independent outsourced competitive analysis / high-level R&D resource. Fortune 500, government agencies and other large IT consumers who are inherently conservative and very careful about making major commitments to new technologies, rising product niches or specific competing products.

The qualifier “quasi” is there because product vendors are also a significant source of revenue for tech analysis firms in the form of conferences tables, speaking opportunities, and other collaborations like cobranded white papers. Much of my last startup’s marketing budget was focused on jumpstarting the legitimacy of our product by participating in exactly such deals. In retrospect, the analysis walks a fine line and is careful to maintain the integrity of their brand above all else. I’m not sure our vendor-analyst partnerships moved the needle more than without them although it did provide some visibility and legitimacy that emerging startups live and die on (engaging in a PR firm was our other major Marketing spend but that’s an entirely different story).

One of the fun aspects of being an analyst must be having the top officers from the leading vendors and cutting edge startups eagerly presenting their best pitch and having a unique birds-eye view of technology. The other fun aspect is writing white papers quantifying the relative state of various key sectors/products and making future predictions with cool graphs like the Gartner Magic Quandrant and Gartner Hype Cycle.
