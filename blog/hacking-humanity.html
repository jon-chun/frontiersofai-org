"May you live in interesting times" is an apocryphal Chinese curse that may originate with Hagel:

"World history is not the ground of happiness. The periods of happiness are empty pages in her."

Today we are definitely living in interesting times. Advances in hardware, algorithms and data have enabled an AI/ML revolution that has finally escaped the economic gravity of government funding to become economically profitable in both mass and niche markets. Concurrently, many related fields like Neuroscience, Cognitive Science, Psychology, Anthropology, Genomics, Material Science, etc. are achieving breakthroughs, many enabled by more powerful computation. Altogether, there is a virtuous reinforcement cycle most clearly seen in rising interdisciplinary fields like Synthetic Biology, Neuromorphic Computing and bolder steps toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

One of the first things a security team should do to harden a computer system against attacks is to minimize the attack surface area hackers could exploit. Use firewalls and routers to close all IP address and ports by default, constantly monitor updates and apply patches immediately, isolate key resources and provided a high-resolution user security hierarchy on a need to know basis, etc. In addition, policies and practices should be in place to perform penetration testing, automate log processing and network monitoring, use automated anomaly detection to catch and shutdown attacks as they are launched etc. While there will always be an arms race between hackers and security experts, history generally favors the informed and conscientious white hats.

Hacking humans is as old as storytelling, myths and magic. It's modern art forms include propaganda, advertising, social psychology and behavioral economics. Unlike computer hacking above, the attack surface to hack humans is large, growing and with small chance of reversing or even achieving equilibrium. Science is creating greater insights into human cognition, emotions and behavior that is going beyond the descriptive and predictive and into the 'nudging' category.
