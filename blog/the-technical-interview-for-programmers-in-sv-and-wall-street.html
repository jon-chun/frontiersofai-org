Created: 2019-09-24 • Status: draft

My first career-track job was working in the financial information industry in Tokyo and my last Silicon Valley startup was primarily funded by hedge funds out of NYC. Beginning in undergrad at Berkeley, many of my more talented STEM friends and co-workers were pulled into finance. A fellow student researcher from Lawrence Berkeley Labs even hosted me in NYC to get me to join him at Goldman where he eventually made partner.

In the past year I've had several students interested in and interview with both Silicon Valley and Financial Companies. While there is more variety and growing opportunity in Silicon Valley for the type of students I teach at a selective Small Liberal Arts College (SLAC) the window of opportunity is narrower in Finance. The one student I had who interned at a hedge fund in NYC this summer did so more due to a constellation of factors unrelated to the classes he took with me.

The appeal of Silicon Valley and Finance for most people is the high-pay and status. Beyond this however is the fact both are hyper-competitive industries where information and technology are core competitive advantages. As a result there is a high concentration of exceptionally bright and creative people working on the most intellectually interesting and technically challenging problems that are changing the world (for better or worse).
