In this, our final week, we look at what some speculate may be humanity’s last great invention: Artificial Intelligence (AI). We’ll see how AI has rapidly grown to be a central focus in academia, industry and even politics. 

We’ll take a brief look at the history of AI, measure the accelerating pace of innovation, and return to questions we raised in the first weeks of class. Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) are introduced, with parallels drawn between both simple and complex models of human neurophysiology. After 60 years of divergence, we’re now seeing a synthesis of approaches and discoveries between AI and neuroscience of the human brain (the most sophisticated model of intelligence in our known universe).

We’ll ask some of the questions with which we began this course in September: What is intelligence? And what is consciousness? Then we’ll consider whether a machine can be either intelligent or conscious. Finally, we’ll consider two very different positions on the rise of AI: the fear of extreme existential risk, as well as the promise of a better, more intelligently-organized world.
