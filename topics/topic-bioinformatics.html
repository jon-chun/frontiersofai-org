Bioinformatics - Hacking Human Biology with Big Data and Computational Models

In our penultimate week we explore a computational interpretation of biology as an information processing system. We'll understand why a computational model of cellular life is, in terms of data, orders of magnitude more complex than any computational or mathematical model we've seen so far. 

We'll also see how the unique challenges of bioinformatics require high-performance computational and large-scale statistical methods to study genomics--one of the original big data applications. Bioinformatics and computational biology have revolutionized our understanding of genetics, medicine and life thanks in large part to rapid advances in computer science: big data, computational power and improved algorithms. 

As in other weeks, we see how rapid progress in technology is poised to challenge the very nature of humanity.  Forty years ago test tube baby technologies like IVF were new, controversial and pushing the limits of medical ethics at the time. Despite initial attempts to limit access to the procedure, today approximately 10% of all births in Denmark are born with assisted reproductive technology like IVF.

The fictional world depicted in the 1997 film Gattaca is fast becoming a reality.  For the cost of a few thousand dollars, parents could practice statistical 'soft' eugenics by fertilizing multiple eggs, testing each for desirable traits across a million SNPs, and selecting only the 'best' one for implantation. How can society prevent the resulting massive, permanent, and accelerating biological inequality irreversibly separating the 'haves' from the 'have-nots'? Other technologies also stand poised to change what it means to be human forever, most notably attempts to "cure" aging. Finally, biotechnology has the potential to bring much good, but it also has the potential to sow much harm through the creation of new and previously unimaginably lethal bioweapons.
