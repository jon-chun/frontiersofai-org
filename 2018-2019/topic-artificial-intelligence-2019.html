Week Fourteen Apr 29, May 1, 3 Artificial Intelligence, Neuroscience and Consciousness
M Class:             Artificial Intelligence and Neuroscience
W Lab:               Deep Neural Nets with Keras/TensorFlow
F Discussion:     Artificial General Intelligence: Promises and Perils

In this, our final week, we look at what some speculate may be humankind’s ‘last great invention‘: Artificial Intelligence. Embodied AI in particular relies upon and can be extended by virtually every technology we covered this semester from data sensors to robotics as well as from algorithms to social networks. In particular, we’ll introduce the three main types of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) built upon a simplistic model of human neurophysiology (the ANN). This week is a preview of our course next semester titled ‘AI for Humanities‘ wherein we take a programmic approach to learning specific AI frameworks and libraries applied to analyzing text, sound, image and film common to the Humanities (with QR credit).

This week we’ll tour a variety of recent AI systems that generate text, music, art and video to survey the recent/current state of the art (SOTA). We’ll also study the history of AI, gauge the arguably accelerating pace of innovation and see some promising new directions in AI research. Along the way we’ll look into concrete concerns about AI safety, ethics and transparency (Fairness, Accuracy and Transparency).

In addition to DNNs, we’ll explore the original inspirations from Neuroscience as well as new opportunities for cross-pollination between the fields of AI and Neuroscience. We’ll see why, after 60 yrs of divergence from the connectome to symbolic AI, we are once again seeing a synthesis of approaches and discoveries between AI and Neuroscience. 

Finally, we’ll venture into the philosophical and speculative with glimpses into the nature of art/creativity, consciousness, brain uploads and safeguarding humanity from AI.
