Course Description:

How do we think of humanity in the digital age? Can we program humanity into our technology by generating music, analyzing vast quantities of literary text, and producing great visual works of art? Or will humans be programmed through genetic engineering, predictive policing, manipulations of social media, and domestic surveillance? Are we firmly entrenched in a Posthumanist world, and is Transhumanism a future to which we should aspire?

This course will bridge the gap between humanities and computational thinking in both a theoretical and practical manner. Each week we’ll present a fundamental technology through homework assignments and hands-on labs. In parallel, we’ll contextualize our understanding of new technologies with discussions of the larger social impact and ethical dilemmas. Weekly programming exercises will familiarize you with the most popular programming language, Python.

The broader goal of the course is to understand technologies driving seismic social change in order to empower you to speak with an informed voice. This is an introductory course with no prerequisites.

By the end of this course, you will:

  • Understand emerging ethical debates surrounding key technologies poised to transform humanity.
  • Gain a foundational understanding of key aspects of our Age of Information from Dataism and Information Theory to Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence.
  • Relate this emerging picture to conversations about Humanism, Posthumanism, and Transhumanism.
  • Understand how new computational tools both challenge and deepen traditional approaches to the Social Sciences, Humanities, and Fine Arts.
  • Have a portfolio of project-based work in Visualization, Data Wrangling and Analysis, and Computational Textual Analysis along with a final project of your choice.

20% Attendance and Discussion

19% Python programming exercises

20% Final Project

Homework:

Each week we will explore a new set of curated multimedia including film clips, news articles, datasets, policies, and opinion pieces. We will be drawing from a wide variety of sources including blog posts, online tutorials, podcasts, videos, and other materials appropriate to the topic at hand. Please consult programminghumanity.wordpress.com under Readings.

Quizzes:

Short quizzes will test comprehension of key terms and information. The lowest three scores will be dropped. You always have the option to write summaries of lectures and readings should you feel that the quizzes haven’t adequately assessed your comprehension. Quizzes cannot be made up if you miss class.

Mini-projects and Final Project:

Three mini-projects will allow you to explore and gain confidence in a number of foundational skills and approaches. For the final project, you will  develop one of the projects begun as a mini-project or propose a new project.The final project should demonstrate a clear understanding of the relevant technology and display in-depth analysis that goes beyond a survey research paper.

Final Thoughts on Grades:

This class is meant to be a fun and exploratory introduction. Students will bring different strengths and backgrounds to this interdisciplinary class, and the emphasis will be on developing these personal interests and competencies. The final grade will be based on showing up engaged and prepared, demonstrating completion of homework through quizzes, and completing mini projects and python programming tutorials that prepare you to complete a final project. If you do the work, you will do well.

Statement of Academic Integrity and Disability Accommodations:

At Kenyon we expect all students, at all times, to submit work that represents the highest standards of academic integrity. It is the responsibility of each student to learn and practice the proper ways of documenting and acknowledging those whose ideas and words they have drawn upon (see Academic Honesty and Questions of Plagiarism in the Course Catalog). Ignorance and carelessness are not excuses for academic dishonesty. If you are uncertain about the expectations for this class, please ask for clarification. Students with disabilities who will be taking this course and may need academic accommodations are encouraged to make an appointment to see me as soon as possible. Also, you are required to register for support services with the Office of Disability Services in the Olin Library, Center for Innovative Pedagogy.
