Bots Robots, Lethal Autonomous Weapons, Brain-Computer Interfaces, Automation/UBI and Ethics
Monday:       Automation, Economics, Soft Automation, Universal Basic Income
Wednesday:  Robotics, Fundamentals, Autonomous Cars, Lethal Autonomous Weapons (LAWs) and Brain-Computer Interface (BCI)
Friday:      Automation and Ethics:  A Peaceful Life of Leisure or a Dystopian Nightmare

This week we begin the last section of our course.  We’ll apply many of the programming, statistical and modeling concepts we’ve already learned to exploring the intersection of technology and humanity in Ro/Botics, Computational Biology/Synthetic Biology and Artificial Intelligence.

You’ll learn about some of the technology underlying robotics including real-time operating systems, computer vision and control theory.  In addition, we’ll introduce some of the unique challenges involved in fusing computational intelligence with physical embodiment. Finally, we’ll look at a variety of robotic applications and associated social consequences: automation -> unemployment/UBI, lethal autonomous weapons -> International Law/Ethics and Brain-Computer Interface/BCI -> engineering evolution and inequality.

Many concepts from previous weeks will come into play when discussing the many implementations and ramifications of automation and ro/bot technologies.  In parallel, many questions about humanity are brought into sharp relief by the rapid progress and introduction of these technologies.

Discussion Questions:

  • AI and robotics researchers often try to anthropomorphize technology via Voice Interfaces (VUI) or biped robots with human faces and gestures.  Often this makes interaction more intuitive and effective.  Are there scenarios where we should design AI or robots to be clearly non-human?  In which scenarios and why?
  • Earlier we read about the influential Libet study that suggests that human free will is more illusionary than real. Perhaps there’s a spectrum across humanity regarding the degree of free will? If robots are developed that can be objectively proven to have more free will than some humans (e.g. addicts or OCD disorders) should they earn rights equivalent to their degree of free will?  Should AI/robotics, even if objectively proven to be just as sentient as humans, forever be enslaved and subjected to dirty, dull and dangerous work in service to humans?  Would there ever be a degree of evolution AI/robotics that would justify them earning some form of human rights?
  • What do you feel about AI/robots developing emotional intelligence?  Humans constantly lie, persuade or otherwise try to influence others for their own benefit; would it be wrong if AI/robots do the same?  Can you think of any rules or regulations (e.g. transparency or liability laws) that could make the use of affective computing tolerable or even preferable to the human variety?
  • Humans have developed a number of laws to protect the most vulnerable of our society (e.g. child services, laws against elder abuse and predatory lending practices). How do we calibrate just how aggressive, intelligent and effective our AI/robotic technologies can be to ensure these legal protections?
  • Former US Justice Dept Lawyer and current Professor John Yoo at Berkeley Law School recently made the argument that we may not want to rule out using assassin drone technology in a first strike scenario.  After all, surgically killing one person or a group of leaders is objectively more humane than the massive loss of life, property and material that comes with conventional warfare.  What do you think of this reasoning?  Can you think of qualifications that could improve this position? Given the growing appeal of automated warfare, can you think of a way to retard the logical slide toward an automated tech warfare that is detached from any public awareness or understanding?
  • Should we allow users of self-driving cars to program their own choice of casualties in the unfortunate instance in which someone might die? Should this be determined by country to account for cultural difference?
  • In a world of automation, is UBI the only way to ensure that all are provided for?
