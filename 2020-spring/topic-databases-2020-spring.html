Databases – Legal Studies
M Class:           Databases and Big Data
W Lab:             RegEx, SQL, and REST/GraphQL
F Discussion:    The Surveillance State:  Privacy vs Security, China vs The West

Data has been called the new oil. So far we’ve studied the fundamental nature, representations, transformations, latent structures and probabilistic character of data through the lenses of philosophy, physics, maths, cognitive science, computer science and statistics. This week addresses the practical concerns of storing, organizing, searching, analyzing, sharing and mutating data.

To this end, we’ll be looking at a family of related technologies that underlie most of the computational infrastructure today: Databases, SQL, RegEx, REST and GraphQL APIs. We’ll see how technologies like these offer a broad range of practical solutions to the many complexities and theoretical trade-offs in how we manage the explosive growth of data by optimizing the balance between speed, accuracy, cost and salability.

Our humanistic enquiry arises from exploring the consequence of ever more larger, capable, inexpensive and ubiquitous data collection systems. In less than a generation oil and gas companies have been displaced by Silicon Valley companies as the world’s most valuable corporations. Data is the one common key asset and competitive advantage all these companies share: Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft and Google. Similarly, governments have been aggressively rolling out public and secret domestic surveillance programs at the expense of individual rights to privacy, threats to freedom of expression and the potential for unheard of abuse by concentrations of power. How can society enjoy the benefits of technology while safeguarding against its abuses?

Questions to Consider for Friday’s Discussion:

  • This week we talked about some unexpected US government surveillance programs.  Had you heard of any of these programs before?  Which ones surprised you the most?  Why?  Had any of your family or friends heard of these programs?  Why do you think there is so little awareness of these programs?
  • Recent Presidential Administrations have reached historic levels of catching and prosecuting government whistleblowers who leak classified information or otherwise run afoul by revealing embarrassing/illegal government activity.  US Intelligence Agencies have even gone so far as to target Congress persons and journalists at the AP with aggressive surveillance to target and unmask specific confidential sources.  This has had a chilling effect on both journalist and whistle-blowers who have been convicted and imprisoned at historic rates.  What do you think has prompted and/or justified this much more aggressive surveillance by the US government over the past several decades?  How would you balance the good of whistleblowers against breaking the law regarding leaking classified information?
  • Do you think the pendulum of government surveillance has swung too far in one direction?  If so (or not), please explain why in as objective and neutral facts/context as possible.  Do you think it may swing back?  Why?  Is there anyway to alter the current trajectory of surveillance trends?
  • No matter what the level of surveillance currently, one major problem is the lack of transparency where the public and elected politicians often seem to not know or be in control of intelligence agencies.  Can you see a way to bring more transparency and accountability to government surveillance programs.  Is there a way to separate legitimate government information needs from overreach while protecting as much privacy as possible.
  • Some claim “Privacy is Dead” and “If you have nothing to hide, you have nothing to fear”.  Do these arguments persuade you?  How much privacy do you feel you should be left in the face of aggressive government and big corporations which desire to collect everything about you?  Are there any areas of your life that you should be able to keep private and government/corporations be forbidden to collect?  For example, healthcare (genomic predispositions to certain conditions or behaviors like MOA/Aggressiveness), intimate relations (voice/text/video -> composite profile) or political leanings (analysis of videos of personal conversations at pubs, gyms, restaurants that help probabilistically reveal your voting history and stance on public policy issues).
  • Once the governments or large corporations have whatever private data you feel comfortable within the previous questions, does your information become their property for them to freely use, model, share and resell to anyone?   Since theft, hacks and data breaches are generally inevitable how could any government/corporation be held liable if your private information becomes public?
  • As we’ve read, most government intelligence agencies claim they need to have a wide and deep surveillance system of everyone at all times to thwart terrorism.  While there has been little to no evidence the greatly expanded surveillance state has prevented much terrorism that wouldn’t have been caught by other means, it does seem to be more effective at catching criminals and government whistleblowers.  Why don’t intelligence agencies cite the enhanced policing abilities rather than speculating on terrorism?  How much of your privacy would you sacrifice to live in a safer world?  How about AI managed cameras everywhere coupled with automated (non-)lethal weapons to neutralize any state threats in real time?   Similarly equipped drones in a mesh network over major urban centers?
  • One of the biggest fears of massive state surveillance is the leverage it gives rulers/leaders a so much control of systems like politics, media and the police that they would become insulated from the will of the people.  Worse, history has shown that bad individuals eventually and regularly make their way into power.  What ways could society design a system to be as free of such abuses as possible?  What specific safeguards, either legally, procedurally and/or technically could we put in place to make sure inevitable bad rulers don’t exploit the surveillance state to create another Caligula, Genghis Khan or Hitler?
