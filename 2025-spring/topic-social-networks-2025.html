Topic: Social Networks 2025

Home » 2025 Spring » Topic: Social Networks 2025
Created: 2025-11-19 • Modified: 2025-12-04

In earlier weeks, we set the groundwork for understanding network and graph theory, as well as rational actor and cooperative game theory. This week we integrate a number of previous concepts to consider the influence of social networks and the ways in which humans operate outside rational actor norms.

We start with a number of ideas that challenge assumptions underlying rational actor behavior: cognitive biases, the power of emotion, and biological aspects of behavior that call into question the very notion of free will itself. 

We'll then consider techniques to capture, model and exploit human emotions and social behavioral patterns using network theory and graph theory. We'll explore at a few examples of social network analysis in TV shows: The Song of Ice and Fire and Friends. In class, we'll do a bit of a deeper dive into exploring social media networks.

All of this background leads up to discussion: are we being hacked by our technology or can we program humanity into our tools? Tristan Harris made his name arguing for a more careful relationship to our tools lest they "hack" us. Yuval Harari also worries about our "hacked" humanity, most notably because the notions of "rational" humanity inherited from the Enlightenment underpin our political theory of democracy. Is this conception of what it means to be human out of date?

This week gives us the final set of tools you'll need to complete your final project. We will be introduced to NetworkX--focus on the concepts, as we will rely on Gemini to help us code your miniproject #3. Remember to have your screenplay or play ready for Tuesday.

As a reminder, here are your choices for a final project. Begin thinking about what you'd like to do, and please check digital kenyon iphs 200 for ideas.

  1. Finding a story in data (using Tableau or Python)
  2. Exploring a text (novels, scripts, social media, news, financial text, etc) using sentiment analysis (in special cases you can also try topic modeling, however you will need to learn the theory behind it)
  3. Social network analysis (again, you may need to spend more time reviewing the various ways to explore networks).
  4. Exploring the ethics of a current technology with detailed attention to the technology itself. NOTE: Earlier feedback on your sentiment analysis and Tableau projects is to guide you on your final projects. Earlier feedback should guide you as to ways you would need to improve were you to select that option for a final project and hope to receive an A.
