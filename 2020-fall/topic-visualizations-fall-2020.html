Visualizing Data and Data-Driven Narratives

After a lightening introduction to the history, philosophy and theory behind computer science, our course now moves onto more practical topics.  This week we’ll work with the most popular commercial data visualization software package called Tableau. This will lay the foundation for your first mini-project. Tableau is one of the best examples of how computational work is evolving towards what is called a “low-code”, “no-code” or “visual programming” style.

The art of storytelling through engaging and interactive visualizations is a valuable skill. So is the ability to critically assess such works and to recognize when people fudge the data to tell a story they want you to believe. After this week concludes on Friday, you’ll have one more week to create and hand in a Tableau visualization that tells a compelling narrative on a topic of your choosing. Hopefully, you’ll be inspired by the many great examples we review this week.

This week we also look at how technology is changing how we are informed about the world through journalism. We’ll discuss some of the challenges and crises journalism faces in the era of big data, automated reporters, mass personalization, and the effects of the emergent military-industrial-information complex.

  • Explain why John Snow is considered one of the first data journalists.
  • How does Data and Data Visualization change/augment traditional journalism? What new skills do journalists need to acquire in the age of data journalism?
  • How do data visualizations and narrative reinforce each other?
  • Given a data visualization, be able to identify the poor design elements, non-compliance with best practices, misleading practices and outright errors.
  • Identify provide a more in-depth interpretation of both the Nicholas Cage and Ali Wong visualizations studied this week.
  • Be able to identify the various abstraction levels in the pyramid of the Grammar of Graphics tutorial from the reading A Comprehensive Guide to the Grammar of Graphics.
  • Be able to label an object hierarchy corresponding to the main components from Tableau Public as presented in class.
  • From the Age of Disinformation Disorder presented in class, know the 7 common forms of information disorder and be able to classify a given piece of Fake News as Disinformation, Misinformation or Malinformation
  • Be able to describe and critique 3 examples of the growing Military-Industrial Complex of BigData Psyops.
  • In the Columbia Journalism Review’s interview, what are the main reasons Snowden gives for why the media increasingly ignores its traditional role of informing the public?
