Created: 2019-11-14 • Status: draft

The first Computer Science diploma was granted at Cambridge University in 1953 and the first CS Department in the US was formed in 1962 at Purdue University. Until relatively recently programming was a discipline anchored in and designed primarily to train students in Engineering, Maths and Physics. The vast majority of programming courses, textbooks and MOOCs reflect this heritage.

Within the last several decades computation has become ubiquitous and spread far beyond the walls of the labs at MIT. The cumulative long-term effects of Moore's Law on compute, Big Data collected from the Internet and IoT and theoretical breakthroughs in areas like Machine Learning are fundamentally reshaping society in profound ways. Digital literacy is acknowledged as key to be able to think critically about, productively participate in and thrive in this 4th Industrial Revolution.

ps nearly 70 years of formal existence, it was firmly grounded in the hard sciences: Engineering, Maths and Science.
