Last week I was speaking to a well placed private wealth fund manager who complained how new MBAs from the top schools all want to go to Silicon Valley instead of investment firms like his. This is not a new complaint, but I thought some of left-coast startup mania would've died down since the 2008 financial crash given that Wall Street has been experiencing one of the longest economic expansion in modern times.

It's perfectly understandable why Silicon Valley would be more appealing than NYC/Boston for the majority of new graduates today: weather, natural beauty, openness, cultural cache, etc. As Silicon Valley matures, it has come to draw upon a far broader pool of talent than just programmers and engineers. However, I expected graduates from top MBA programs to be more narrowly focused on the economic upside which seem to clearly favor finance of startups. I assume these MBA students are proficient at modeling and optimizing expected lifetime risk-adjusted income so why where they leaving substantial amounts of money on their lifetime earnings table?

The odds of hitting it big in the Silicon Valley startup octagon are probably on par with hitting it big in Hollywood or pro sports - in other words not good. Most startups fail to find funding and of those that do my guess is 90% fail. Of those that don't fail, the founders are usually diluted down to a single digit equity stake if they can resist being replaced by more experienced/proven talent from their VCs' networks. Alas, typical acquisition prices are usually too low to make this amount to much in Silicon Valley. The entire endeavor sounds like pitching a script in Hollywood expecting it to become the next "Big Bang Theory". Similar to actors and athletes, most SV entrepreneurs also have a relatively short career window unlike in finance which generally become more lucrative with time, experience and networking.
