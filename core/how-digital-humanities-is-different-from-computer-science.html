How Digital Humanities is Different from Computer Science Draft

Home » Core » How Digital Humanities is Different from Computer Science
Created: 2019-05-04 • Status: draft

Our DH courses take an natural language immersion approach to learning basic Python programming. Instead of a traditional Python programming class, our students learn enough to understand basic code, parse out algorithmic thinking, integrate and manipulate various data sources, rely on popular libraries as well as intelligently communicate with CS domain experts. We recommendation students to continue their study of Python and programming to systematically and thoroughly learn the power, expressiveness and art of programming in Python.

Our DH courses are designed with three facts in mind. First, the vast majority of students are not necessarily interested in programming qua programming which ends up excluding or marginalizing many groups. Our course is a Humanities-first approach designed to abstract programming details where possible to demonstrate the beauty, power and social relevance of technology thus creating the interests and incentives to learn programming.

Secondly, we realize the vast majority of our students will not become programmers yet will increasingly interact with technology. Our students need to be able to think critically about, communicate effectively about and comfortably interact with concepts, tools and team specialist across a wide variety of technologies. This
