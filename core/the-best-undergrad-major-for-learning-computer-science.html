Created: 2019-07-18 • Status: draft

Any 'Best' claim is really just the solution to an optimization problem that heavily depends upon identifying and quantifying key parameters like 'best', 'learn' and 'computer science'. Key parameters can also be unstated latent factors like individual motivation, experiences as well as future academic or career aspirations. Still, there do seem strong trends that suggest that majors outside of Computer Science are often more suitable for many students.

The biggest confusion in addressing this question is the overloaded term "Computer Science" which can lead to very different paths like (a) traditional software engineering, (b) machine learning/data science and (c) theoretical computer science. Traditional software engineering is the most recognizable in the form of traditional programming: enterprise software development, web stack or mobile apps for example. ML/Data Science is more statistical and mathematical analysis endemic to most white collar work or supporting knowledge workers from doctors to sales pipelines. Theoretical CS is the most rarefied breed using the most abstract thinking for fields like database design or cryptography.

One metric of 'best' undergrad preparation for CS is the major that gives you the most flexibility to move freely between the various manifestations of CS both within academia and beyond. As the field of CS and the tools are rapidly changing, you also have to take into account how priorities are shifting.
