2019 Spring

This year marks the first iteration of selecting students for our Digital Humanities Research Fellowship funded by the National Endowment of the Humanities through the NEH Distinguished Teaching Professorship. This represents a unique opportunity for both faculty and students to empower traditional humanities scholarship with computational approaches.

This spring our DH Research Fellows will participate in our first Multidisciplinary Workshop on Computational Textual Analysis. Under the direction of Professor Elkins and Professor Chun, students will consult with faculty members and help them apply several Natural Language Processing techniques to primary textual sources.

We currently expect a wide range of academic texts including those from the fields of:

  • English
  • History
  • Political Science
  • Philosophy
  • Classics
  • Religious Studies
  • Modern Languages and Literatures

The Natural Language Processing techniques we’ll explore will include:

  • Information Retrieval
  • Computational Linguistics
  • Sentiment Analysis
  • Topic Modeling
  • Semantic Embeddings

Students will learn or reinforce their knowledge of these computational approaches to textual analysis. Both students and faculty will be introduced to the best-of-breed tools and libraries through the application of NLP to an interesting and diverse body of textual scholarship.

In advance of our Multidisciplinary Workshop on Computational Textual Analysis, our Fellows will review and consolidate their knowledge of NLP and selected tools/libraries under the direction of Professor Chun. Fellows will have an unique opportunity to consult with faculty across campus on cutting edge research projects.

During the workshop led by Professor Elkins, Fellows will assist faculty in exploring and analyzing their text.
